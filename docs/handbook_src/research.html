DocketPilot

An Agentic AI System for Indian Legal Research, Drafting, Anti-Hallucination Verification and Case Management

DocketPilot reads an advocate's case papers, finds the governing statute sections and judgments, drafts documents, and checks every citation against a store of real judgments before the advocate sees it. Client names are masked on the advocate's machine before any text reaches the language model. Hearing dates read from the papers go onto a built-in calendar.

Students: Srigaran N and team. Supervisor: Dr. Saravanan R. Code: github.com/srigaran2020/DocketPilot (private).

The seven agents

A LangGraph StateGraph in docketpilot/agents/graph.py; each step is a function in docketpilot/agents/nodes.py. State is checkpointed in PostgreSQL after every step, so a paused or interrupted draft resumes. The graph pauses at approval until the advocate decides.

Ask DocketPilot request path

One chat routes to every feature by rules, not by the LLM (docketpilot/api/assistant.py): dates (answered from the calendar, no AI), schedule, draft, verify, similar cases, risk & strategy, otherwise a verified answer (docketpilot/chat.py).

  1. Mask every turn of the conversation with one shared placeholder map.
  2. Resolve the case: a case number or party name in the message selects the right case file.
  3. Retrieve up to 6 judgment passages (hybrid + rerank) and up to 5 statute sections; fit them into Groq's request budget (decision 31).
  4. The model answers only from tagged sources; tags become real citations (swap_tags).
  5. Verify every citation and section, auto-correct, restore real names, show one quiet line: "✓ 4 checked · 1 corrected".

Verification and auto-correction

docketpilot/verification/: extract.py (find citations, quotes, section references), engine.py (steps 2-9), quotes.py, correct.py (auto-correction).

  1. Extract citations with case names, direct quotes and "Section N of <Act>" references (79 Acts + repealed IPC/CrPC/Evidence Act).
  2. Normalise each citation to a canonical key.
  3. Resolve against 52,166 cases (exact key, then name + year).
  4. Verdict: VERIFIED, PARTIAL, CONFLICT (cites a different case) or NOT_FOUND.
  5. Quotes matched against the judgment text (exact / near-exact / not found / headnote only).
  6. AI judge: does the passage support the proposition? Separate strict prompt, 2 at a time.
  7. Good law signals: later treatment found in the citation graph.
  8. Statutes: section exists, in force on the relevant date, quoted text identical; repealed codes flagged after 1 July 2024.
  9. Gate: red items block approval; amber items need sign-off.

Auto-correction then replaces a wrong citation with the real one when the named case exists, removes citations whose case is missing, whose quote is not in the judgment or that do not support the point (with the sentence that relied on them), and removes case names written without any citation.

Privacy gateway

Three layers, all on the advocate's machine (docketpilot/privacy/):

  • Redaction: rule recognisers for Aadhaar (Verhoeff checksum), PAN, phone, IFSC, bank account, vehicle number, address, plus spaCy en_core_web_sm for names and organisations.
  • Masking: consistent placeholders (PERSON_1 is the same person in every paper of a case), reviewed by the advocate before saving.
  • Encryption: the placeholder → real-name map is stored per case in a Fernet-encrypted vault (vault/<case>.vault). Real names return only on screen and in the approved Word file.

Figures for the paper

Files in reports/figures/ as 600 dpi PNG and SVG, sized for one IEEE column (3.5 in). Suggested captions: reports/figures/captions.md. Regenerate with the commands below after re-running the benchmark.

How this comparison was made
  • Metric: cost reduction = 1 - (system's cost / its baseline's cost), each against the baseline its own authors chose.
  • DocketPilot: 8 legal research questions across practice areas (bail, divorce, specific performance, limitation, arbitration, maintenance, consumer, motor accident). DocketPilot retrieves 6 reranked judgment passages and up to 5 statute sections, answers, then checks every citation (including AI-judge calls). Every token was counted from Groq's API responses and priced at the list price of gpt-oss-120b (US$0.15 per million input, US$0.60 per million output tokens). Baseline: the same model given the full text of the same judgments and the same statute sections; its input was counted with the o200k tokenizer and its output assumed equal to DocketPilot's answer, which favours the baseline. Result: US$0.00285 vs US$0.02215 per answer.
  • LegalMind: published figure (IEEE Access, 2025): an agentic framework built on a fine-tuned DeepSeek R1 with a structured legal dataset, reporting 42.6% cost reduction over baseline AI models. Not re-run by us; the abstract does not give its task set or baseline in detail.
  • NAVEMBED: published figure, same paper as NAVINDEX (Hany et al., arXiv:2607.05764, 2026), run RUN-005: embedding retrieval with reranking instead of injecting the whole document corpus into the context; 15 questions; US$0.611 vs US$1.881 recorded dollar cost, i.e. 67.5% lower. Same type of baseline as DocketPilot.
  • NAVINDEX: published figure (Hany et al., arXiv:2607.05764, 2026): transactional legal documents; the model navigates a compact structured index instead of having the whole document corpus injected into its context on every query. 25% lower dollar cost, with answers judged tied with full injection on all 18 document-bound questions. Same type of baseline as DocketPilot (whole documents in context).
How this comparison was made
  • Metric: recall = share of wrong citations the system flags; precision = share of its flags that really are wrong; F1 = their harmonic mean. Same definitions for every bar.
  • DocketPilot: 150 real Supreme Court of India cases; 756 wrong citations generated from them (wrong page, wrong year, swapped party names, wrong court, invented case, citation that does not support the stated proposition) mixed with 156 correct citations (seed 13). Each was run through DocketPilot's verifier against its store of 52,166 cases. Recall 99.6%, precision 99.3%.
  • Claude Code, GPT-5, Qwen3.6-27B, GPT-OSS-120B, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Qwen3-8B: published figures (Liu, Stammbach & Henderson, arXiv:2606.21155, 2026, Table 2): excerpts of US legal briefs with injected citation errors drawn from a taxonomy of 1,000+ real court filings with fabricated citations; 390-example test set. Each model ran as a tool-using agent (up to 30 steps) that can look up case law; Claude Code used its own agent harness. Agentic results shown.
  • Same LLM: GPT-OSS-120B is the model DocketPilot uses, so the gap between its bar and DocketPilot's shows what the verification pipeline adds over the model alone.
  • Caveat: same metric, different data: US briefs vs Indian Supreme Court citations; their errors include subtler types.

Limitations and future work

  • No lawyer reviewed the legal data, rules or templates; all marked unverified.
  • Held-out PII leakage is 3.2% against a 1% target; the advocate's preview is the safeguard.
  • Benchmarks are small (8 questions, 8 judgments) because of the Groq free tier (8,000 tokens/minute, 200,000/day per key).
  • Coverage: SC full text 2015-2026 and five HC benches 2022-2025; IPC, CrPC and the old Evidence Act are absent from India Code's API.
  • No OCR for scanned papers; Word output only.
  • Database is not encrypted at rest; no HTTPS until hosted (planned: Oracle Cloud Always Free with Docker Compose).
  • Strategy advice never predicts outcomes.
